# 08. Scenario and HR Questions

[Back to README](../README.md)

24 questions.

### Q1. Tell me about yourself (for a fresher tester).

Give a short summary: your education, your testing skills (SDLC, STLC, test cases, bug reporting, Jira, basic API testing), one project you tested and why you want a QA role. Keep it under a minute.

### Q2. Why do you want to be a software tester?

Because I like finding problems, thinking from the user's side and helping a team ship something reliable.

### Q3. What is your biggest strength as a tester?

Pick one real strength, such as attention to detail, and give a short example.

### Q4. What will you do if a developer disagrees with your bug?

Stay calm, show clear evidence and the requirement, discuss facts not opinions, and involve the product owner if needed.

### Q5. What will you do when you find a critical bug just before release?

Report it immediately with evidence, assess impact with the team, and let the stakeholders decide to fix, delay or release with known risk.

### Q6. What if requirements are unclear or missing?

Ask the BA or product owner, document the answers, and do not assume. Raise the gap early.

### Q7. What will you do if you have little time to test?

Prioritize by risk and critical business flows, run smoke and high priority tests first, and tell stakeholders what was not covered.

### Q8. How do you handle a bug found in production?

Reproduce, log with full details and severity, inform the team, help with a hotfix if needed and add a regression test.

### Q9. How do you decide when to stop testing?

When exit criteria are met: planned tests executed, no open critical defects, coverage and pass rate targets reached, or time and budget run out with risks agreed.

### Q10. How do you test without requirements documents?

Talk to stakeholders, study similar products, use exploratory testing, and document what you learn as you go.

### Q11. What would you do on your first day in a new project?

Understand the product, read requirements, set up the environment and tools, meet the team and run basic flows.

### Q12. How do you keep learning in testing?

Practice on demo sites, read blogs, build small projects, learn automation and take part in testing communities.

### Q13. What is the difference between a tester and a developer's mindset?

A developer builds to make it work. A tester questions how it may fail and checks the edges.

### Q14. What is a test summary report?

A report of test results at the end of a cycle: executed, passed, failed, blocked, defects by severity and recommendation.

### Q15. What is a blocked test case?

A test that cannot be run because of an external issue, such as a bug or missing environment.

### Q16. What is a skipped test case?

A test intentionally not executed in this cycle, with a recorded reason.

### Q17. How do you test a feature you do not understand?

Read the docs, ask questions, explore the feature, take notes and then design tests.

### Q18. What would you do if you were asked to test a website with no test cases?

Do exploratory testing first, learn key flows, write test cases from what you find, and prioritize the critical paths.

### Q19. How do you deal with pressure and deadlines?

Plan and prioritize, communicate early about risks, focus on critical features and ask for help when needed.

### Q20. What is the most important quality of a test case?

It must be clear and repeatable, with a precise expected result, so anyone can run it and get the same outcome.

### Q21. Describe a bug you found. (Fresher answer approach)

Use a real bug from a practice project: what you did, what you expected, what happened, severity, and how it was fixed. Never invent one.

### Q22. What are your salary expectations?

Research the market range for fresher QA roles in your city, give a reasonable range and say you are open to discussion based on role and learning.

### Q23. Where do you see yourself in five years?

A skilled QA professional with automation experience, maybe moving toward senior QA or SDET responsibilities.

### Q24. Do you prefer manual or automation testing?

Be honest. Say manual testing builds a strong base and you are learning automation to grow.
